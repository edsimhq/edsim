Three Ideas to Watch For

These ideas didn't arrive with the Declaration of Independence in 1776 — colonists had been practicing them for over 150 years. Learn the three colors below; you'll see them again in the Documents tab.

Popular Sovereignty and Consent of the Governed are closely linked: sovereignty is the claim that power belongs to the people, and consent is how people actually put that power into action — by electing leaders, swearing an oath, or agreeing to a compact. They're worth knowing as separate ideas, but in the Documents tab you'll identify them together as one "self-rule" idea, since a single sentence rarely shows one without the other. Limited Government is the one that's easy to tell apart — that's the real contrast in this activity.
