Security v. Freedom: Find It in the Text

Contemporary Issues — matching ideas to the real words that state them

Directions: Learn the key words. Then, for each document, read the lettered sentences from the real text. For each plain-language idea, pick the letter of the sentence that says that idea. Use "Check My Answers" to see how you did — you can try again if you get one wrong.

Step 1: Learn the Words First

⚔️
Authorize
To officially give permission for something to happen.
🕵️
Surveillance
Watching or monitoring people, often without them knowing.
📜
Warrant
Official permission from a judge to search someone or something.
🛡️
National Security
Keeping the country and its people safe from danger.
⚖️
Civil Liberties
Basic freedoms protected from government interference — like privacy.
🏛️
Congress
The part of government (Senate + House) that makes laws.
Quick reminder
🛡️ Security documents
Give the government MORE power to act.
⚖️ Rights documents
LIMIT what the government is allowed to do.

Step 2: Find It in the Text

⚖️

U.S. Constitution & 4th Amendment 1787 / 1791

The Real Text
ATo declare War, grant Letters of Marque and Reprisal, and make Rules concerning Captures on Land and Water;
BThe President shall be Commander in Chief of the Army and Navy of the United States.
CThe right of the people to be secure in their persons, houses, papers, and effects, against unreasonable searches and seizures, shall not be violated,
Dand no Warrants shall issue, but upon probable cause, supported by Oath or affirmation, and particularly describing the place to be searched, and the persons or things to be seized.
1. The President is Commander in Chief — leads the military.
2. Congress has the power to declare war and control the military.
3. People have a right to be free from government searches.
4. A judge must approve a warrant — there has to be a good reason.
🛡️

AUMF — Authorization for Use of Military Force Sept. 18, 2001

The Real Text
AWhereas, on September 11, 2001, acts of treacherous violence were committed against the United States and its citizens;
BResolved by the Senate and House of Representatives of the United States of America in Congress assembled...
CThe President is authorized to use all necessary and appropriate force against those nations, organizations, or persons he determines planned, authorized, committed, or aided the terrorist attacks that occurred on September 11, 2001,
Dor harbored such organizations or persons, in order to prevent any future acts of international terrorism against the United States by such nations, organizations or persons.
1. Congress gave the President power to use the military against anyone connected to the 9/11 attacks.
2. This included groups or countries that helped plan or hide the attackers.
3. This is a resolution passed by Congress — not a formal declaration of war.
🛡️

President Bush's Address to Congress Sept. 20, 2001

The Real Text
AOur war on terror begins with al Qaeda, but it does not end there. It will not end until every terrorist group of global reach has been found, stopped and defeated.
BWe will direct every resource at our command—every means of diplomacy, every tool of intelligence, every instrument of law enforcement, every financial influence, and every necessary weapon of war—to the destruction and to the defeat of the global terror network.
CSo tonight I announce the creation of a Cabinet-level position reporting directly to me—the Office of Homeland Security.
DWe will come together to give law enforcement the additional tools it needs to track down terror here at home.
1. Bush named al-Qaeda as the enemy and said the fight would not stop there.
2. The U.S. would use every tool — military, money, law enforcement, intelligence.
3. Bush announced a brand-new Cabinet position: Homeland Security.
4. Bush asked for new legal tools to track terrorists inside the U.S.
🛡️

USA PATRIOT Act (DOJ Summary) Oct. 26, 2001

The Real Text
AA roving wiretap can be authorized by a federal judge to apply to a particular suspect, rather than a particular phone or communications device.
BAllows federal agents to ask a court for an order to obtain business records in national security terrorism cases.
CThe Act removed the major legal barriers that prevented the law enforcement, intelligence, and national defense communities from talking and coordinating their work.
DAllows law enforcement officials to obtain a search warrant anywhere a terrorist-related activity occurred, instead of needing a separate warrant in every district.
1. Police can use wiretaps that follow a specific person, not just one phone.
2. Federal agents can get business records to investigate terrorism.
3. Government agencies can share information with each other more easily.
4. Law enforcement can get one search warrant that works across more than one area.
