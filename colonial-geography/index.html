Geography & Colonial Economies: Card Match Investigation

Welcome

Learning goal: You will examine the impact of geographic factors on patterns of settlement and the development of colonial economic systems in the New England, Middle, and Southern colonies.

Here's what's in each tab and about how long it should take:

  1. Directions (you are here) — 2 minutes.
  2. Prediction — Read three regions' geography without knowing which region is which, and predict what economy would develop in each. 8–10 minutes.
  3. Card Match — Drag or click 9 economic activity cards onto the 3 region cards where you think each one best fits. 10–15 minutes.
  4. Justify — For each region, explain in writing why its geography caused those economic activities. 10–15 minutes.
  5. Wrap-Up — Compare your work to a sample answer key, reflect, and export your work. 5 minutes.

Your work saves automatically as you go on this device. When you're done, use the Export button on the last tab to save a copy of everything you wrote.

Part 1: Blind Prediction

Read the three geography profiles below. You are not told which colonial region each one describes yet.

Mystery Region A

Rocky, thin soil that resists large-scale farming. Cold winters and a short growing season. A jagged coastline with dozens of natural harbors. Dense forests inland. Rich fishing grounds just offshore.

Mystery Region B

Fertile soil, especially in wide river valleys. Moderate climate with a long growing season. Major rivers reaching deep inland, ideal for moving goods.

Mystery Region C

Warm climate with a long growing season. Flat, fertile lowlands well-suited to large fields. A long coastline and many navigable rivers reaching far inland.

You'll find out which regions these were — and check your predictions — in the Card Match tab.

Part 4: Wrap-Up

Compare your predictions and matches to one reasonable answer key. If yours is different, that's not automatically wrong — go back and strengthen your justification if you can defend it.

New England: Shipbuilding, Fishing & Whaling, Subsistence Farming & Small Trades — poor soil and a short growing season ruled out large-scale farming, so colonists turned to the sea and small trades instead.

Middle Colonies: Grain Farming, Milling & Flour Production, River & Port Trade — fertile river-valley soil and a long growing season made large-scale grain farming possible, and rivers moved the surplus to port.

Southern Colonies: Cash Crop Plantations, Livestock & Mixed Farming (Backcountry), River Export Trade — warm climate and fertile lowlands favored large cash-crop plantations along navigable rivers, while inland areas away from the coast relied on livestock and mixed farming.
